# What-If AI — Tough Q&A Prep (10 Judge Questions, 3 Sentences Each)

> **Human Editing Notice**: Rehearse and paraphrase these 3-sentence responses in your own voice so both team members answer crisply within the 5-minute Q&A window.

### Q1. Causality vs. Correlation: "How do you prove your elasticity isn't fake correlation from holiday spikes or unobserved shocks?"
We explicitly disclose on every assumption screen, narrative summary, and slide that our elasticity estimates are controlled observational estimates, never proven causality. Our Spec-C Poisson GLM narrows confounding by controlling for Vietnamese Lunar New Year windows, double-day events (`9.9` to `12.12`), paydays, promotions, and zero-stockout censoring — recovering confounding bias from `-5.9` to `-1.8` on simulated `11.11` events (true `-1.5`). Where historical variation stays blind to unobserved store shocks or price flatness, we trigger automatic `low_price_variation` warnings and surface full bootstrap uncertainty bands so managers test small A/B price steps rather than treating point forecasts as guarantees.

### Q2. Data Privacy: "Retailers guard prices and supplier costs closely — does uploading CSVs leak rows to OpenAI or Gemini?"
Never: uploaded CSV tables stay strictly in session memory during runtime, are never persisted to browser `localStorage`, and no raw transaction row or customer identifier is ever transmitted to any LLM. Optional LLM calls during ambiguous column mapping inspect only column headers and up to five digit-masked samples (`####`), while NLQ parsing sees only SKU IDs and product names, and the explainer sees aggregated simulation totals verified by regex post-checking. For privacy-sensitive pilots or air-gapped stores, the entire platform runs 100% deterministically offline with zero external API keys configured.

### Q3. Why Not ChatGPT or Excel?: "Why wouldn't a shop manager just export their CSV to Excel or paste it into ChatGPT?"
Excel gives deterministic arithmetic on historical numbers but leaves store managers guessing price elasticity, bootstrap confidence ranges, and multi-constraint Reverse What-If frontiers manually across formulas. Meanwhile, general chatbots have no structural econometric engine, leak raw spreadsheets via prompts, and routinely hallucinate arithmetic when aggregating receipts or computing compounded log-elasticity margins. What-If AI locks econometrics and Monte Carlo inside verified Python and Go engines, guaranteeing zero hallucinated digits while automating messy Vietnamese CSV normalization (`33/33` benchmark fields accurate).

### Q4. First Paying Customer & Pricing: "Who is your exact first paying customer, and how do you price this?"
Our initial paying beachhead is independent Vietnamese multi-SKU retail shops and small omnichannel brands (`20–300 SKUs`, exporting sales from POS tools like KiotViet, Sapo, or Excel) who run frequent promotional markdowns and lack dedicated pricing analysts. After proving lift during a free 60-day shadow pilot, we target a simple SaaS tier aligned with SME POS add-ons — affordable monthly pricing per workspace rather than enterprise consulting fees — keeping compute costs near zero because fitting and Go simulation run cleanly on commodity CPU servers. Realized ROI is measured directly against trailing naive pricing decisions during the pilot rather than assumed upfront.

### Q5. Accuracy & Honest Misses: "Your backtest beat the baseline 18/19 times — what broke on the 19th case, and how reliable is this?"
On our 19-event out-of-time backtest (trained strictly prior to each historical price shift), Spec-C beat the 14-day trailing naive baseline 18 times (`94.7%`) with `88.7%` average 80%-interval coverage, and synthetic ground-truth recovery MAE stayed between `0.048` and `0.118`. The single miss occurred on `grocery_fmcg_15` `P04` during a `2025-11-11` (`-28.7%`) flash sale (model WAPE `20.6%` vs. naive `17.5%`), where an extreme multiplicative promotional shock saturated daily variance — though our `80%` uncertainty interval still achieved `100.0%` coverage of actuals. Showing both wins and misses teaches managers to rely on prediction intervals during mega-sale anomalies rather than blindly trusting point predictions.

### Q6. Flat-Price SKUs: "What happens if a convenience store rarely changes prices so your regression has no price variation?"
Observational regression cannot extract elasticity out of flat history, so we never fake precision on constant-price items. Whenever historical `sd(ln price)` falls below `0.04`, our engine explicitly attaches a `low_price_variation` reliability warning — proven by flagging **8 out of 8 (100%)** flat-price SKUs in our `hard_low_price_variation_8` stress dataset (where unwarned MAE degrades to `0.580`). In the UI, these warning chips alert managers to inspect wider uncertainty bands or run a small controlled price test before relying on Reverse optimization.

### Q7. Foreign Public Data (M5 / Dunnhumby): "Why reference US Walmart or Dunnhumby loaders if you're targeting Vietnamese retailers?"
We never claim American consumer elasticities represent Vietnamese shoppers; instead, foreign public panels serve strictly as methodological stress tests on real non-synthetic price and calendar noise. Our `m5_loader` and `dunnhumby_loader` transform real external retail transactions into our canonical schema (stripping household IDs cleanly and labeling `PUBLIC (M5)` / `PUBLIC (DUNNHUMBY)` with illustrative `25.000 VND/$` conversion tooltips). That proves our pipeline isn't overfitted to synthetic scripts, while a live Vietnamese shop always estimates elasticity exclusively from its own uploaded CSV (`YOUR DATA`).

### Q8. Missing Costs & Messy Export Formats: "Many Vietnamese SMB spreadsheets track selling price and quantity, but omit unit cost (`gia_von`) or repeat receipt lines — does your pipeline crash?"
No — during ingestion (`ml-py/app/ingest/transform.py`), receipt-level rows sharing `(date, product_id)` are automatically aggregated to daily volume and quantity-weighted unit prices before modeling (`100%` tested on receipt variants). When `unit_cost` is missing, the Confirm Columns screen explicitly prompts the manager for an editable fallback `margin_pct` assumption and carries clear warnings into profit simulations so margin dependence stays transparent. Similarly, missing stock columns disable inventory capping gracefully unless the user sets a manual stock ceiling on Screen 3.

### Q9. Multi-SKU Cannibalization & Competitor Reaction: "What if lowering Shirt A's price steals sales from Shirt B or triggers a competitor price cut?"
Currently in MVP (`docs/LIMITS.md`), each SKU is modeled independently without estimating a full cross-price cannibalization matrix or competitor game-theory response over the 7–90 day horizon. Including cross-elasticity without rich simultaneous variation often overfits noisy small-N store datasets, so we chose reliable single-SKU own-price estimation plus hard margin/stock constraints as the disciplined v0.1 foundation. In the post-pilot roadmap, category-level cross-price terms and manual competitor-price covariates (already previewed in our mapping design) will extend multi-SKU basket optimization once retailers connect wider transaction histories.

### Q10. Why Go + Python + React?: "Isn't maintaining both Go and Python overkill for a 2-person student hackathon team?"
Separating Python (`ml-py`: Pandas/Statsmodels ingestion, Vietnamese calendar econometrics, NLQ guardrails) from Go (`engine-go`: stateless Monte Carlo + grid optimizer + HTTP gateway) gives us both scientific library maturity and blazing execution speed (`38.8 ms` for 101 grid candidates x 5,000 draws, `100%` deterministic across core counts). Better yet, both tiers share frozen JSON schema contracts (`contracts/`), zero runtime state in Go, and zero business math in React — allowing both developers to build and test independently in parallel. Finally, if Wi-Fi drops or an API fails during live demos, deterministic rule parsers and precomputed Go fixture snapshots keep the entire 6-screen workflow responsive offline.
